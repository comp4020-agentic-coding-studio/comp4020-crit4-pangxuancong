# Crit 4 — An instrument

**The breakthrough** was realising that working with an AI agent needs a
complete prompt plan, not a loose idea. Telling Claude "I want to build
something like this" without pinning down the interaction model, the tech
stack, or the concrete design references produces something that runs but
isn't good — the gaps just aren't the kind a check can catch. My first attempt
at this instrument worked that way and I threw it out. FLUID worked because I
did the opposite: I worked out the product first, referenced the interaction
and visual design I actually wanted, and only then turned it into a full plan
for the agent to build against.

**What this changed** about who I want to be as a developer: I need a clear
sense of roughly what the product should look and feel like before I ask for
it, not just a vague direction — the plan is only as good as the product sense
behind it. And I need to keep developing that sense horizontally, across
stacks and mediums, rather than staying deep in one — this week's audio and
shader work isn't something I'd have had good instincts for without having
gone and looked at how the effect I wanted is actually built.
